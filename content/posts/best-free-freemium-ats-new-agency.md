Free ATS tiers are almost always built to convert you to a paid plan the moment your agency has more than one person touching the system — the real question is which free tier gets you the furthest before that wall shows up.

> **The short answer**
>
> **[Loxo](https://loxo.co)'s free tier** is the strongest genuine option — a full ATS/CRM at $0, not a crippled trial, but capped at one user, which makes it a solo-recruiter tool rather than a team one.

## What "free" actually means across vendors

Most recruiting software companies don't offer a real free tier — they offer a time-limited free trial (usually 14-30 days) that converts to a paid plan automatically, or requires a sales call to continue. Loxo is the notable exception: its free plan is the actual ATS and Recruiting CRM, unlimited jobs and projects, plus a Chrome sourcing extension — permanently, not for a trial window. The catch is a hard cap at one user, so it works for a solo recruiter or a founder validating the business before hiring, but stops being usable the moment a second person needs access.

**[Recruit CRM](https://recruitcrm.io?fpr=esteban-fe19e2)** and Bullhorn don't publish permanent free tiers — both offer free trials of their paid plans instead, which is a different thing: useful for evaluation, not for running an agency at zero cost long-term.

## Who a free tier actually makes sense for

- **A solo recruiter or founder pre-revenue.** Loxo's free tier removes software cost entirely while you validate whether the business works, which matters when every dollar of runway counts.
- **Testing before committing budget.** Even if you expect to outgrow it in months, running real candidates and clients through a genuinely free tier tells you more about fit than a 14-day trial does.

## Where free tiers stop making sense

The moment a second recruiter joins, or you need client-facing views, deeper reporting, or job board distribution beyond the basics, free tiers reach their ceiling fast — and the upgrade jump from Loxo's free tier to its Basic plan (~$169/user/month) is a bigger leap than Recruit CRM's paid entry tier (~$85-95/user/month), so "start free, upgrade later" isn't automatically the cheapest long-run path if you expect to add headcount soon.

<div class="label">Bottom line</div>
<p>Free tiers make sense for solo, pre-revenue use — but check the upgrade price before you build your workflow around one, since the jump to paid isn't always small.</p>
