These terms get used almost interchangeably in staffing software marketing, which is confusing, because they originally describe two different jobs.

## What each term originally meant

An **ATS (applicant tracking system)** tracks candidates through a hiring pipeline — sourced, screened, interviewed, offered, placed. It's built around the candidate as the central record.

A **CRM (candidate/client relationship management)** — in a staffing context, this usually means managing the *client* relationship: who you're working with, what roles they've opened, what you promised them, when to follow up. It's built around the client and job order as the central record.

## Why the line has mostly disappeared

In staffing and recruiting specifically, nearly every modern platform — Recruit CRM, Bullhorn, JobAdder, Loxo — combines both functions in one system, because the two jobs are genuinely intertwined at a staffing agency: the same person is often sourcing a candidate and managing the client who has the opening. Splitting those into two separate tools that don't talk to each other creates exactly the kind of friction a small agency can't absorb.

So when a vendor's marketing page says "ATS" and another says "CRM" or "recruitment CRM," they're often describing very similar combined platforms — the labeling difference is more about which side of the workflow the vendor wants to emphasize in its pitch than a real functional split.

## What actually varies between platforms

Since most tools now do both, the more useful comparison points are:

- **Which side is more fully built out.** Some platforms have deeper candidate-sourcing and parsing tools; others have stronger client-side pipeline and reporting. Neither is universally "better" — it depends on whether your bottleneck is finding candidates or managing client relationships.
- **How the two sides connect.** Look at how a platform links a candidate record to the job order and client it's associated with — a clean link here is what actually saves time day to day, more than which term is in the marketing copy.
- **Whether it's staffing-specific or general-purpose.** A generic sales CRM adapted for recruiting will handle the client side well but often lacks staffing-specific candidate workflow (resume parsing, pipeline stages built for hiring rather than sales). Tools built specifically for staffing and recruiting agencies tend to handle both sides more naturally.

When you're evaluating a platform, it's worth ignoring whether it calls itself an "ATS" or a "CRM" entirely, and instead checking directly whether it handles both the candidate pipeline and the client relationship in one connected system — that's what the terminology is really trying, imperfectly, to describe.
